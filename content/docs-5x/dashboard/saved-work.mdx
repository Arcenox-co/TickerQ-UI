---
title: "Drafts, templates, and activation"
description: "Save unfinished one-time work, reuse complete chain designs, and activate a reviewed draft exactly once."
---

The dashboard's **Saved work** panel keeps configuration separately from scheduler execution rows. A saved definition contains a job or chain design; it has no execution status, lock, result, or log history.

Saved work is a side panel, not a sidebar view. Open it with **Drafts & templates** on the Time Tickers page, which shows **Drafts** and **Templates** tabs, or with **Templates** on the Cron Tickers page, which lists cron-chain templates. Both buttons are hidden for read-only users. Inside the chain builder, **Use Templates** opens a separate picker with your saved templates for that target plus built-in starter layouts.

## Choose a draft or template

| Saved item | Use it for | Scheduler behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time draft | An unfinished one-time job or chain | Cannot be acquired until you activate it |
| Time-chain template | Reusing a complete one-time workflow design | Opens a new unsaved copy in the builder |
| Cron-chain template | Reusing a recurring workflow design | Opens a new unsaved cron configuration |

Single jobs are quick to recreate, so the dashboard does not offer a single-job template. A single time job can still be saved as a **draft** when it needs later review.

## Save and activate a time draft

Choose **Save draft** in the one-time job form or chain builder to retain the configuration without publishing a runnable ticker. When you leave unsaved one-time work, the leave prompt also offers **Save as draft**. The draft keeps its execution time and, unless you clear **Include request payloads**, its request data. Open it later from **Saved work**, select **Edit** to review the complete graph in the builder, and choose **Review activation**. The same button is available on the draft's card in the panel; it reads **Resume** while an earlier activation is still pending.

**Review activation** opens the **Activate draft** dialog. Choose **Run now** and confirm with **Activate now**, or choose **Schedule for**, keep or change the prefilled execution time, and confirm with **Activate and schedule**. Confirming publishes the complete time-ticker graph once. The store takes a durable, versioned activation claim before it creates the root ticker, so two dashboard replicas cannot activate the same draft twice. After activation, the dashboard opens the new job in Time Tickers, and the draft's card shows **Activated** with a **View job** link.

A draft is not an `Idle` job and does not appear in the time-ticker or execution tables before activation.

## Save and edit a chain template

Use **Save template** on a configured one-time or recurring chain. As with drafts, **Include request payloads** controls whether request data is saved; clear it when the structure is reusable but the payload contains customer data or secrets. Saved items without payloads show **payloads excluded** in the panel.

Templates preserve the graph shape, schedule settings relevant to their target, retry/timeout settings, conditions, and descriptions. On a template card, **Use template** opens a new unsaved copy in the chain builder; **Edit** opens the template itself, and **Save template changes** saves it back. Updates and deletes use the stored version so one operator cannot silently overwrite another operator's newer edit.

Template names are unique inside one application namespace and scheduler target. Comparison ignores case, Unicode compatibility differences, and repeated whitespace. Draft display names do not have this uniqueness requirement. The Saved work list supports search and creation-date ordering.

## Assistant-created drafts

When the assistant proposes a one-time job or one-time chain, the card uses **Save draft**. Saving does not schedule the job. The saved card shows **Draft saved** and exposes **Open draft**, where an operator can edit the request, execution time, conditions, and graph before activation.

Recurring schedule proposals still use an explicit confirmation card because their result is a cron definition rather than a time draft. Read [AI assistant](/docs/5.x/dashboard/ai-assistant) for proposal history and authorization behavior.

## Storage and provider behavior

All built-in providers support saved definitions:

- EF Core maps `TickerQDefinitions`; generate and apply an application-owned migration.
- MongoDB provisions a saved-definition collection and indexes.
- Redis stores definitions under the current application partition.
- The in-memory provider loses saved work when the process exits.

Definitions are scoped to the configured application namespace. Custom providers opt in through `ITickerDefinitionStore`. A provider without that capability can continue scheduling, but the Saved work feature is unavailable.

Templates and drafts are not job history and are not removed by job-retention sweeps. Delete them explicitly when they are no longer useful.
