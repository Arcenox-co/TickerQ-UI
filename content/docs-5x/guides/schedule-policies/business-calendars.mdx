title: "Business calendars"
description: "Define reusable schedule policies that decide when time tickers, cron tickers, and chains may start."

A schedule policy is a named, reusable calendar. It decides whether work may start at a given
instant, evaluated in the policy's own time zone. Use it to keep business hours, closure dates, and
holiday rules out of job handlers.

Policies are scoped by `UseApplicationNamespace(...)` and stored by the active persistence provider.
The built-in in-memory, Entity Framework Core, MongoDB, and Redis providers support them.

To control what happens when allowed work starts late, read
[late starts and catch-up](/docs/5.x/guides/schedule-policies/late-starts). That rule works with or
without a calendar.

## Define and seed a policy

Use `UseSchedulePolicySeeder` for policies owned by the deployed application. `UpsertAsync` makes
reconciliation idempotent. Policy seeding runs before time- and cron-ticker seeders, so those
seeders can reference the policy safely.

```csharp
using TickerQ.Utilities.Models;

builder.Services.AddTickerQ(options => options
    .UseApplicationNamespace("orders-production")
    .UseReconciliationEpoch(1)
    .UseSchedulePolicySeeder(async (policies, cancellationToken) =>
    {
        await policies.UpsertAsync(
            SchedulePolicy.Create("EU business hours")
                .WithDescription("Weekdays from 09:00 to 17:00")
                .InTimeZone("Europe/Tirane")
                .AllowWeekdays(
                    DayOfWeek.Monday,
                    DayOfWeek.Tuesday,
                    DayOfWeek.Wednesday,
                    DayOfWeek.Thursday,
                    DayOfWeek.Friday)
                .AllowBetween(new TimeOnly(9, 0), new TimeOnly(17, 0))
                .ExcludeAnnualDate(1, 1)
                .ExcludeAnnualDate(12, 25)
                .ExcludeDate(new DateOnly(2027, 1, 2))
                .Build(),
            cancellationToken);
    }));
```

The seeder runs only while a new [reconciliation epoch](/docs/5.x/guides/configuration#reconciliation-epoch)
activates. A restart with the same, already-activated epoch skips every seeder, so a changed policy
definition is not applied. Advance `UseReconciliationEpoch(...)` when you change a seeded policy in
code.

## How a policy evaluates an instant

TickerQ converts the instant to the policy's time zone and checks the rules in this order. The first
rule that blocks the instant decides the result and its reason.

| Rule | Builder method | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekday | `AllowWeekdays(...)` | The local day must be one of the allowed days. All days are allowed by default. |
| Daily window | `AllowBetween(start, end)` | The local time must fall inside at least one window. With no window, the whole day is allowed. |
| Exact date | `ExcludeDate(date)` | Blocks one local calendar date. |
| Annual date | `ExcludeAnnualDate(month, day)` | Blocks the same month and day every year. |
| Date provider | `ExcludeDatesFrom(name)` | Blocks dates that a registered `IScheduleDateProvider` excludes. |
| Composition | `Require(name)` / `ExceptWhen(name)` | Combines the result with other policies. See [compose policies](#compose-policies). |

A window's start is inclusive and its end is exclusive. A window whose end is before its start
crosses midnight. Equal start and end allows the whole local day. The time zone defaults to `UTC`
when none is set.

Create and update validate the policy. They reject an unknown time zone, a policy with no allowed
weekday, an invalid annual date, and a date provider that is not registered.

## Names and revisions

Policy names are stable identifiers. Matching ignores case and repeated whitespace, while the
display spelling is kept. Schedules and composed policies reference the name, so a name cannot be
changed after creation. Create a replacement policy and move its usages when a name must change.

Each policy also has a revision number, shown as a badge such as **v3** in the dashboard. It
increments when the rules change. Updates pass the revision they read, so one editor cannot
overwrite another editor's newer change. Each schedule also records the revision it was validated
against.

## Apply a policy to one-time work

Use a `TimeSchedule` to choose the policy and what happens when the requested time is blocked:

```csharp
var result = await timeTickerManager.AddAsync<PrepareInvoicesJob>(
    TimeSchedule.At(DateTimeOffset.Parse("2026-12-25T10:00:00+01:00"))
        .UsingPolicy("EU business hours")
        .WhenBlocked(BlockedOccurrenceBehavior.MoveForward),
    cancellationToken);
```

| Behavior | Dashboard label | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Reject` | Reject creation | Creation or update fails and nothing is persisted. This is the default. |
| `Skip` | Save as skipped | The root and its descendants are stored as `Skipped`. No function runs. |
| `MoveForward` | Move forward | The job moves to the next allowed instant. Creation fails if none exists within about ten years. |

TickerQ keeps the requested time, the effective execution time, the adjustment reason, the policy
name and revision, and the blocked behavior. The policy is evaluated when the job is created or when
its time, policy, or blocked behavior is updated. Editing the policy later does not move or skip a
one-time job that was already admitted.

### Chains

Set the policy on the chain root. TickerQ rejects a policy on a child step.

```csharp
var result = await timeTickerManager.AddChainAsync(chain => chain
    .RunAt(DateTime.UtcNow.AddHours(1))
    .UsingPolicy("EU business hours")
    .WhenBlocked(BlockedOccurrenceBehavior.MoveForward)
    .StartWith<PrepareInvoicesJob>()
    .Then<SendInvoicesJob>(), cancellationToken);
```

The policy only decides when the root may start. After the chain starts, child steps follow their
run conditions, delays, and retries even if the policy window closes. A workflow therefore never
pauses halfway through its normal or compensation path.

## Apply a policy to recurring work

TickerQ generates cron candidates in the cron definition's time zone, then checks each one against
the policy. Blocked candidates are omitted, so cron policies always skip. `Reject` and
`MoveForward` apply only to one-time jobs.

```csharp
var result = await cronTickerManager.AddAsync<GenerateDailyReportJob>(
    CronSchedule.From("0 9 * * 1-5")
        .InTimeZone("Europe/Tirane")
        .UsingPolicy("EU business hours"),
    cancellationToken);
```

Recurring chains set the policy on the root:

```csharp
var result = await cronTickerManager.AddChainAsync(chain => chain
    .WithExpression("0 9 * * 1-5")
    .InTimeZone("Europe/Tirane")
    .UsingPolicy("EU business hours")
    .StartWith<PrepareInvoicesJob>()
    .Then<SendInvoicesJob>(), cancellationToken);
```

The cron and policy time zones may differ on purpose. For example, a schedule can produce a 09:00
New York candidate and still check it against a European closure calendar.

Future cron candidates always use the current stored policy, so editing a policy changes upcoming
cron runs. Creation and update fail when the expression has no allowed future occurrence under the
policy.

## Compose policies

Build a larger rule from smaller policies:

```csharp
await policies.UpsertAsync(
    SchedulePolicy.Create("Production window")
        .InTimeZone("Europe/Tirane")
        .AllowWeekdays(
            DayOfWeek.Monday,
            DayOfWeek.Tuesday,
            DayOfWeek.Wednesday,
            DayOfWeek.Thursday,
            DayOfWeek.Friday)
        .AllowBetween(new TimeOnly(7, 0), new TimeOnly(22, 0))
        .Require("Company calendar")
        .ExceptWhen("Maintenance freeze")
        .Build(), cancellationToken);
```

- `Require(name)` allows an instant only when the referenced policy also allows it.
- `ExceptWhen(name)` blocks an instant whenever the referenced policy allows it.
- Create and update reject a missing reference, a dependency cycle, and a policy that both requires
  and excludes the same policy.

## Register date providers

Use an `IScheduleDateProvider` when exclusions come from application code or a local cache:

```csharp
using TickerQ.Utilities.Interfaces;

public sealed class PublicHolidayProvider : IScheduleDateProvider
{
    public string Name => "Public holidays";

    public bool IsExcluded(DateOnly localDate)
        => localDate is { Month: 1, Day: 1 } or { Month: 12, Day: 25 };

    public string GetReason(DateOnly localDate)
        => $"{localDate:yyyy-MM-dd} is a public holiday.";
}

builder.Services.AddTickerQ(options => options
    .UseApplicationNamespace("orders-production")
    .UseReconciliationEpoch(1)
    .AddScheduleDateProvider<PublicHolidayProvider>()
    .UseSchedulePolicySeeder((policies, cancellationToken) =>
        policies.UpsertAsync(
            SchedulePolicy.Create("Company calendar")
                .ExcludeDatesFrom("Public holidays")
                .Build(), cancellationToken)));
```

`GetReason` is optional. Without it, the reason is "Excluded by" followed by the provider name.

`IsExcluded` runs synchronously while TickerQ searches candidate instants. Keep it fast,
deterministic, and free of network calls. Refresh remote holiday data into a local cache outside the
provider.

Register the same provider name and behavior on every scheduler node. A node that lacks a referenced
provider blocks every instant for that policy.

## Manage policies in the dashboard

Open **Policies → Schedule Policies** in the dashboard sidebar.

<DashboardScreenshot name="schedule-policies" alt="Schedule Policies page with equal cards showing time zones, allowed days, daily windows, exclusions, and composition" caption="Each card highlights the allowed days and strikes through the blocked ones. Requires and Except tags show composition." />

- **Cards** show each policy's revision, time zone, allowed days, daily windows, exclusion counts,
  and any `Require` or `ExceptWhen` references.
- **The editor** groups settings into **Basics**, **Availability**, **Exceptions**, and **Combine
  policies**. It lists the registered date providers to choose from. When editing, it shows how many
  time and cron tickers use the policy, and the name field is locked.
- **Combine policies** sets each other policy to **Not used**, **Require**, or **Exclude**.
  **Require** matches `Require(name)` and **Exclude** matches `ExceptWhen(name)`.
- **Preview** on a saved policy takes a cron expression and evaluates its next 20 candidates. It
  shows how many are allowed and blocked, and marks each one **Allowed** or **Blocked** with the
  reason, in the policy's time zone. Quick buttons fill in common expressions.
- **Delete** is rejected while a ticker or another policy still references the policy.

The [AI assistant](/docs/5.x/dashboard/ai-assistant#schedule-policies) can also list policies,
preview a cron expression against one, and draft a new policy or a change for you to confirm.

Time and cron creation panels select a policy under **Advanced settings → Scheduling**. In the
chain builder, the root step has its own **Scheduling** section. When a one-time job is moved by its
policy, its details show the original **Requested** time and the schedule adjustment reason.

**Run now**, re-run, and chain retry are explicit operator actions. They start the work immediately
and do not check the calendar. Disabling a cron still stops its future runs even when the policy
would allow them.

## Manage policies from code

Inject `ISchedulePolicyManager` to list, create, update, delete, evaluate, and preview policies. See

## Storage and deployment

The EF Core model always includes the `SchedulePolicies` table and the policy columns, even when no
policy is configured. An existing database still needs an application-owned migration before the new
binary starts. MongoDB creates its namespace and name index during startup. Redis uses
namespace-scoped keys and needs no schema step.

Custom persistence providers must implement `ISchedulePolicyStore`. Without it, schedules that do
not use policies keep working, while policy operations fail with an explicit error. Read
[schedule policy storage](/docs/5.x/guides/storage-upgrade#add-schedule-policy-storage) before
deploying to an existing store.
