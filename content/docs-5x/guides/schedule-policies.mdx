---
title: "Schedule policies"
description: "Apply reusable business calendars, time windows, and late-start rules to one-time and recurring work."
---

Schedule policies are named, reusable availability rules for time tickers, cron tickers, and their
chains. They answer whether work may start at a candidate instant in the policy's local time zone.
This keeps business hours, closure dates, and holiday rules out of job handlers.

Policies are scoped by `UseApplicationNamespace(...)` and stored by the active persistence provider.
The built-in in-memory, Entity Framework Core, MongoDB, and Redis providers support them. In the
dashboard, open **Policies → Schedule Policies** to create, preview, edit, and remove policies.

## Define and seed a policy

Use `UseSchedulePolicySeeder` for definitions owned by the deployed application. `UpsertAsync`
makes reconciliation idempotent and runs before time- and cron-ticker seeders:

```csharp
using TickerQ.Utilities.Models;

builder.Services.AddTickerQ(options => options
    .UseApplicationNamespace("orders-production")
    .UseSchedulePolicySeeder(async (policies, cancellationToken) =>
    {
        await policies.UpsertAsync(
            SchedulePolicy.Create("EU business hours")
                .WithDescription("Weekdays from 09:00 to 17:00")
                .InTimeZone("Europe/Tirane")
                .AllowWeekdays(
                    DayOfWeek.Monday,
                    DayOfWeek.Tuesday,
                    DayOfWeek.Wednesday,
                    DayOfWeek.Thursday,
                    DayOfWeek.Friday)
                .AllowBetween(new TimeOnly(9, 0), new TimeOnly(17, 0))
                .ExcludeAnnualDate(1, 1)
                .ExcludeAnnualDate(12, 25)
                .ExcludeDate(new DateOnly(2027, 1, 2))
                .Build(),
            cancellationToken);
    }));
```

`AllowBetween` has an inclusive start and exclusive end. A window whose end is before its start
crosses midnight. Equal start and end allows the complete local day. When no window is configured,
the selected weekdays remain available for the whole day.

Policy names are stable identifiers. Matching ignores case and repeated whitespace, while the
display spelling is retained. Existing schedules and composed policies reference the name, so the
dashboard does not allow renaming it. Create a replacement policy and move its usages when a name
must change. Deletion is also rejected while a schedule or another policy references it.

The version badge on each policy card, such as **v3**, is a revision number and is independent of
the name. It increments when the policy rules change, prevents one editor from overwriting another
editor's newer changes, and records which revision a schedule observed when it was created or
updated.

## Apply a policy to one-time work

Use a `TimeSchedule` to choose both the policy and what happens when the requested time is blocked:

```csharp
var result = await timeTickerManager.AddAsync<PrepareInvoicesJob>(
    TimeSchedule.At(DateTimeOffset.Parse("2026-12-25T10:00:00+01:00"))
        .UsingPolicy("EU business hours")
        .WhenBlocked(BlockedOccurrenceBehavior.MoveForward),
    cancellationToken);
```

The same settings belong on a chain root:

```csharp
var result = await timeTickerManager.AddChainAsync(chain => chain
    .RunAt(DateTime.UtcNow.AddHours(1))
    .UsingPolicy("EU business hours")
    .WhenBlocked(BlockedOccurrenceBehavior.MoveForward)
    .StartWith<PrepareInvoicesJob>()
    .Then<SendInvoicesJob>(), cancellationToken);
```

| Behavior | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `Reject` | Creation or update fails and nothing is persisted. This is the default. |
| `Skip` | The root and its descendants are stored as `Skipped` evidence. No function runs. |
| `MoveForward` | The requested time is retained for audit and execution moves to the next allowed instant. |

The policy is evaluated only for the root's admission. After a chain starts, its descendants follow
their run conditions, delays, and retries even if the policy window closes. This prevents a workflow
from pausing halfway through its normal or compensation path.

TickerQ retains the requested time, effective execution time, adjustment reason, policy name,
revision, and blocked behavior. Editing the policy later changes future decisions; it does not
rewrite a one-time ticker that was already moved or skipped.

## Apply a policy to recurring work

Cron candidates are generated in the cron definition's time zone and then evaluated by the policy.
Blocked candidates are omitted, so cron policies always use skip behavior:

```csharp
var result = await cronTickerManager.AddAsync<GenerateDailyReportJob>(
    CronSchedule.From("0 9 * * 1-5")
        .InTimeZone("Europe/Tirane")
        .UsingPolicy("EU business hours"),
    cancellationToken);
```

Recurring chains use the same root-level rule:

```csharp
var result = await cronTickerManager.AddChainAsync(chain => chain
    .WithExpression("0 9 * * 1-5")
    .InTimeZone("Europe/Tirane")
    .UsingPolicy("EU business hours")
    .StartWith<PrepareInvoicesJob>()
    .Then<SendInvoicesJob>(), cancellationToken);
```

The cron and policy time zones may differ deliberately. For example, a schedule can produce a
09:00 New York candidate and still evaluate it against a European closure calendar. Future cron
candidates always use the current stored policy. The cron row retains the policy revision last
validated by a create or update operation for diagnostics.

## Control late starts and downtime catch-up

Availability and lateness answer separate questions:

- A schedule policy decides whether the requested instant is part of the allowed calendar.
- A missed-execution policy decides whether allowed work is still useful when it starts late.

The durable default is `RunOnce`: persisted work executes once when capacity returns, however late
that is. Use `SkipAfter` when stale work would be harmful:

```csharp
await timeTickerManager.AddAsync<RefreshExchangeRatesJob>(
    TimeSchedule.At(DateTimeOffset.UtcNow.AddMinutes(5))
        .WhenMissed(MissedExecutionPolicy.SkipAfter(TimeSpan.FromMinutes(2))),
    cancellationToken);
```

When the job is more than two minutes late as execution is about to enter user code, it becomes
`Skipped`. For a time chain, the complete graph settles as skipped. A delay exactly equal to the
configured limit may still start. Skipping does not consume a retry because user code did not run.

Cron schedules have two independent controls:

```csharp
await cronTickerManager.AddAsync<RefreshExchangeRatesJob>(
    CronSchedule.From("*/5 * * * *")
        .WhenMissed(MissedExecutionPolicy.SkipAfter(TimeSpan.FromMinutes(2)))
        .CatchUp(CronCatchUpMode.RunLatest),
    cancellationToken);
```

| Setting | Controls | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `WhenMissed(...)` | A persisted occurrence that reaches execution late because of capacity, a store outage, or restart. | `RunOnce()` |
| `CatchUp(...)` | Cron slots that passed while no scheduler was available to persist them. | `Skip` |

`RunLatest` creates at most the latest missed slot that is still inside the `SkipAfter` window. It
does not replay every interval from an outage. A bounded `SkipAfter` rule is required; create or
update rejects `RunLatest` without one. Custom persistence providers must explicitly support cron
catch-up.

## Compose reusable policies

Small policies can be combined into a larger operating rule:

```csharp
await policies.UpsertAsync(
    SchedulePolicy.Create("Production window")
        .InTimeZone("Europe/Tirane")
        .AllowWeekdays(
            DayOfWeek.Monday,
            DayOfWeek.Tuesday,
            DayOfWeek.Wednesday,
            DayOfWeek.Thursday,
            DayOfWeek.Friday)
        .AllowBetween(new TimeOnly(7, 0), new TimeOnly(22, 0))
        .Require("Company calendar")
        .ExceptWhen("Maintenance freeze")
        .Build(), cancellationToken);
```

- `Require(name)` allows an instant only when both policies allow it.
- `ExceptWhen(name)` blocks an instant whenever the referenced policy allows it.
- Missing references and dependency cycles are rejected during create or update.

## Register application date providers

Use an `IScheduleDateProvider` when exclusions come from application code or a bounded local cache:

```csharp
using TickerQ.Utilities.Interfaces;

public sealed class PublicHolidayProvider : IScheduleDateProvider
{
    public string Name => "Public holidays";

    public bool IsExcluded(DateOnly localDate)
        => localDate is { Month: 1, Day: 1 } or { Month: 12, Day: 25 };

    public string GetReason(DateOnly localDate)
        => $"{localDate:yyyy-MM-dd} is a public holiday.";
}

builder.Services.AddTickerQ(options => options
    .AddScheduleDateProvider<PublicHolidayProvider>()
    .UseSchedulePolicySeeder((policies, cancellationToken) =>
        policies.UpsertAsync(
            SchedulePolicy.Create("Company calendar")
                .ExcludeDatesFrom("Public holidays")
                .Build(), cancellationToken)));
```

Registered providers appear in the dashboard policy editor. `IsExcluded` runs synchronously while
TickerQ searches candidate instants, so keep it fast, deterministic, and free of network calls.
Refresh remote data into a local immutable cache outside the evaluator, and register the same
provider name and behavior on every scheduler node.

## Dashboard workflow

Open **Policies → Schedule Policies** in the sidebar. Each policy card shows its revision, time
zone, allowed days, daily windows, exclusion counts, and any `Require`/`ExceptWhen` composition.
The editor groups settings into **Basics**, **Availability**, **Exceptions**, and **Combine
policies**, and lists registered date providers to choose from.

Select **Preview** on a saved policy and enter a cron expression to evaluate its next 20 candidates.
Each candidate is marked **Allowed** or **Blocked** with the rule that decided it. Delete is
rejected while a schedule or another policy still references the policy.

Job creation and edit panels show these controls under **Advanced settings**:

| Group | Time ticker | Cron ticker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduling | Schedule policy, **If blocked**, **If late**, and grace period | Schedule policy, **Late start**, **Catch-up**, and grace period |
| Execution | Timeout | **Skip if already running** and timeout |
| Recovery | Owner loss | Owner loss |

A chain applies these settings to its root step. The cron time zone is set beside the expression.

Run now, re-run, and retry are explicit operator actions. They bypass calendar admission while
preserving their normal occurrence or execution identity rules. Disabling a cron still prevents
future scheduling even when its policy would allow the candidate.

## Persistence and deployment

The EF Core model always includes the `SchedulePolicies` table and policy/missed-execution columns,
even when no policy is configured. This lets an application enable policies later without changing
TickerQ registration, but an existing database still needs the additive migration before deploying
the new binary. MongoDB provisions the namespace/name index; Redis uses namespace-scoped keys.

Custom providers must implement `ISchedulePolicyStore`. Without that capability, schedules that do
not use policies continue to work, while policy operations fail explicitly. See the
[storage upgrade reference](/docs/5.x/guides/storage-upgrade#add-schedule-policy-storage) for the
required relational shape and rollout notes.

The policy model is Native AOT compatible. Application request and result payloads still require
their normal source-generated JSON metadata; see [Native AOT](/docs/5.x/guides/native-aot).
